What KEE prevents us from losing

Four New York City examples show what can be lost when a knowledge system collapses disagreement, scope, provenance, or time into a single answer.

These examples are framed against KEE v0.10.0, but the downstream NYC fixtures were created under KEE v0.7.2 plus ADR-0049. This publication does not claim that the downstream NYC corpus conforms to KEE v0.10.0.

A disagreement can be preserved without turning source prestige into truth

What can go wrong: A naive system can either discard one source or treat every cross-source difference as a contradiction.

What the governed record preserves: The useful governance result is preservation of the disagreement and its provenance. The historical contradiction edge is itself a governed classification, not proof that the records are logically incompatible.

A source is not the same thing as correctness.

  • Overture Maps recorded Brooklyn Botanic Garden with the website https://linktr.ee/brooklynbotanic and coordinates (40.6686244, -73.96442177). Source: source ↗
  • OpenStreetMap way 157016745 recorded Brooklyn Botanic Garden with website https://www.bbg.org and centroid (40.6677056, -73.9646509). Source: source ↗
  • The historical governed fixture classified the two assertion records as contradicting each other. Source: source ↗

Limitations

  • The Overture record cites publisher documentation rather than the exact retrieved feature.
  • The historical contradiction classification remains challengeable.

An apparent contradiction can expose a modeling problem

What can go wrong: A naive system can treat different field values as proof that one source is wrong without checking whether the values answer different scoped questions.

What the governed record preserves: The same historical fixture explains that a public-facing entrance address and a tax-lot address can both plausibly be legitimate for one building. This is a mixed result: the preserved context lets us challenge the fixture's own contradiction classification instead of silently canonizing it.

A governed contradiction marker is still a claim that can itself be challenged.

  • NYC's Library facilities dataset gives Brooklyn Central Library the address 10 Grand Army Plaza and BBL 3011830002. Source: source ↗
  • PLUTO for BBL 3011830002 gives address 415 Flatbush Avenue and owner BROOKLYN PUBLIC LIBRARY. Source: source ↗
  • The historical governed fixture marks the facilities and PLUTO assertion records with a contradiction relation. Source: source ↗

Limitations

  • This is intentionally not presented as a clean KEE success story.
  • The case study does not silently rewrite the historical downstream fixture.

More representations do not necessarily mean more independent evidence

What can go wrong: A naive system can count every representation as a separate vote and overstate evidential support.

What the governed record preserves: Representation count and independent-provenance count answer different questions. Derived products can add value without becoming independent corroboration.

More records do not necessarily mean more independent evidence.

  • The governed NYC Boundaries public view counts 46 active boundary representations across six publisher families. Source: source ↗
  • Only DCP and the Census Gazetteer are included in that artifact's independent land-area comparison. Source: source ↗
  • geoBoundaries and Who's On First remain in the Census provenance cluster, while Overture representations remain in the OpenStreetMap provenance cluster for the relevant source lineage. Source: source ↗

Limitations

  • Independence is purpose-specific; a derived product may still add useful identifiers, hierarchy, packaging, or accessibility.

A newer observation can replace an older one without erasing history

What can go wrong: A naive system can overwrite old observations or compare changed measurement vocabularies as if they meant exactly the same thing.

What the governed record preserves: Currentness, historical validity, assertion time, capture time, and supersession are different dimensions. The three censuses also use different condition/status vocabularies, so raw totals must not be silently treated as directly comparable living-tree counts.

Time is part of the knowledge.

  • The 1995 NYC Street Tree Census fixture records 516,989 street-tree records and is governed as superseded by the 2005 record. Source: source ↗
  • The 2005 fixture records 592,372 street-tree records and is governed as superseded by the 2015 record. Source: source ↗
  • The 2015 fixture records 683,788 street-tree records, including 652,173 with status Alive, and is governed as the active record in this chain. Source: source ↗

Limitations

  • The raw census totals are not presented as directly comparable living-tree counts.
  • The 2005 governed source records a disclosed upstream borough-name data-quality defect.

Governance and limitations

  • The Brooklyn Central Library case is intentionally a mixed result: the historical fixture records a contradiction edge even though its own analysis says both addresses can plausibly be legitimate.
  • The Brooklyn Botanic Garden contradiction edge is a governed historical classification, not proof of logical incompatibility.
  • More representations are not automatically more independent evidence.
  • Time roles remain separate: represented assertion time, capture time, lifecycle state, and supersession are not collapsed.
