One city, several valid boundaries

A boundary depends on the question being asked. The Department of City Planning publishes both water-excluded shoreline geometry and water-included geometry. The Census Bureau publishes county land and water measurements. OpenStreetMap represents each borough and its corresponding county as separate administrative relations, even where they refer to the same real-world extent. geoBoundaries republishes generalized, shoreline-clipped Census-derived county geometry; Overture Maps integrates the exact OpenStreetMap relation versions already governed here while adding stable division and area identifiers. Who's On First adds stable gazetteer identifiers, hierarchy and concordances for the five counties; its default county geometry is a Census-sourced Quattroshapes derivative.

These are not interchangeable records. This view keeps the source, method, administrative framing and review assessment attached to each claim.

Differences that should remain visible

Shoreline vs. offshore

Water-excluded geometry traces inlets, coves and piers. Water-included geometry follows a smoother offshore boundary. A larger area can therefore have a shorter perimeter. The review records this as a methodological distinction, not conflicting evidence.

Borough vs. county

OpenStreetMap keeps separate borough and county relations. The paired records are treated as close representations of the same referent, not collapsed into an exact-identity claim and not marked as a contradiction.

Published fixture and artifacts

The DCP water-excluded and water-included geometries, five Census Gazetteer county measurement records, ten exact OpenStreetMap relation-version metadata records, five geoBoundaries county geometries, five Who's On First county geometries, and eleven Overture Maps division-area geometries are downloadable public fixtures. The comparison map overlays the DCP geometries without a network request. The OpenStreetMap export contains metadata only—not geometry, relation members, Nominatim bounds, centroids, or numeric area. The geoBoundaries export contains the retained generalized, shoreline-clipped 2018 Census-derived geometries with CC BY 4.0 attribution and no numeric area. The Who's On First export contains the retained current Quattroshapes-derived county geometries with stable WOF identifiers, Census GEOID/HASC concordances, and a Shapely-derived geom:area_square_m field disclosed as non-independent. The Overture export contains eleven division-area geometries re-verified directly against the pinned release at publication time, including both of Richmond County's land and maritime representations, with ODbL attribution and no numeric area.

Validation and provenance

Source last-updated values are now backed by primary publisher metadata: Socrata data-update timestamps for DCP, the HTTP Last-Modified value for the Census distribution, and exact OpenStreetMap relation versions. The retained DCP water-included response was captured on August 9, 2026; capture, source update, review and publication remain distinct events.

  • Both public fixtures require exactly five unique borough identities and non-empty geometries.
  • The retained water-included API response regenerates the public fixture byte for byte.
  • Both SVGs regenerate byte for byte from public fixtures without a network request.
  • The source snapshot, fixtures, builders, generators and SVGs are covered by SHA-256 checksums.
  • The retained geoBoundaries subset regenerates as a schema-valid five-feature fixture with byte-identical geometry coordinates, deterministic borough ordering, attribution and limitations.
  • The retained Who's On First subset regenerates as a schema-valid five-feature fixture with byte-identical Quattroshapes geometry coordinates, deterministic borough ordering, Census GEOID/HASC concordances and a disclosed non-independent geom:area_square_m field.
  • The Overture subset regenerates as a schema-valid eleven-feature fixture with byte-identical geometry re-queried directly from the pinned release, exactly one land and one maritime representation for Richmond County, and deterministic borough ordering.
  • The source comparison now covers 46 active governed records: 10 DCP, 5 Census Gazetteer, 10 OpenStreetMap, 5 geoBoundaries, 11 Overture and 5 Who's On First representations.
  • geoBoundaries and Who's On First remain in the Census provenance cluster, and Overture remains in the OpenStreetMap provenance cluster; none increases the independent-source count.
  • The source comparison contains no recorded contradiction: observed differences are assessed as methodological choices, administrative framing or ordinary measurement variance.
  • KnoEdg.NYC publishes this view; each claim continues to identify its applicable public evidence source.

Known limitations

The original DCP API response used for the first map was not retained with a checksum, and the initial geographic-to-SVG projection script was not preserved. Reproducibility therefore begins with the reviewed public SVG-path fixture, not the original API response. Future boundary publications are required to retain the complete response and transformation chain before first publication.

The water-included publication corrects that practice prospectively: its exact API response, checksum, normalized fixture, fixed projection and generated overlay are retained. The Census records are published as measurements without geometry. The OpenStreetMap records are published as exact relation-version metadata without geometry, relation members, Nominatim bounds, centroids, or numeric area. The geoBoundaries publication contains retained generalized, shoreline-clipped 2018 Census-derived geometry with no calculated area; it is not suitable for precise analysis or legal coastal extent. The Who's On First publication contains retained current Quattroshapes-derived geometry that is not an independently surveyed boundary, plus a Shapely-derived geom:area_square_m field that is neither land-only nor independent corroboration. The Overture publication contains geometry that is Overture's own integration of the cited OpenStreetMap relation, not an independently surveyed boundary; its land/maritime classification was re-verified directly against the pinned release at publication time and was found to differ from this collection's original governed disposition for ten of the eleven representations. A follow-up root-cause investigation found the underlying release asset unchanged since the original capture and concluded the original capture was the error; the governed source was corrected accordingly rather than left standing.
