Inside the Map of 30 Immigrant Enclaves

A map titled “NEW YORK CITY IMMIGRANT ENCLAVES” lists 30 entries and does not include Little Italy. The map was published by New York City Tourism + Conventions ↗, and the map itself cites the NYC Mayor’s Office of Immigrant Affairs as its data source. No source in this collection identifies the map’s designer.

The Mayor’s Office of Immigrant Affairs launched an Immigrant Enclave Illustration Series on April 17, 2023. In its end-of-tenure report ↗ covering through December 31, 2025, the office states that 23 illustrations were published. Comparing that report with this map shows five names that appear on the map but not in the report: Little Egypt, Little Odessa, Little Palestine, Little Poland and Little Senegal.

On July 10, 2026, amNewYork ↗ reported that the mayor’s team had updated the map. At the same press conference, the mayor said the map had been created by the prior administration in 2023 and that future changes would include adding Little Italy. As of August 20, no amended version had been located, and the known map file remained byte-for-byte unchanged from an archived copy captured before the July reporting.

How the map compares with an earlier city series

The Mayor’s Office of Immigrant Affairs launched the Immigrant Enclave Illustration Series on April 17, 2023. In its end-of-tenure report ↗ covering through December 31, 2025, the office states that 23 illustrations were published. “Little Italy” does not appear in the 85-page report.

Five names appear on the map but nowhere in that report: Little Egypt, Little Odessa, Little Palestine, Little Poland and Little Senegal. The map therefore includes five names not found in the report.

Two reported criteria

Two source accounts describe different criteria for why a neighborhood might appear on the map. Neither appears in a published selection rule.

In a July 9, 2026 Washington Examiner article ↗, a city spokesperson said the map highlights neighborhoods with substantial foreign-born populations. This describes a demographic criterion based on current population.

The Blaze ↗ quoted people who described a neighborhood’s history of immigrant settlement as a basis for inclusion. This describes a heritage criterion that does not depend on the current foreign-born share.

The two criteria ask different questions. A neighborhood can meet either, both or neither; the evidence does not establish that either criterion was the rule used to make the map.

A separate Department of City Planning threshold

The Department of City Planning’s The Newest New Yorkers 2026, Chapter 3 ↗ defines a “major immigrant neighborhood” for that report as one whose foreign-born density exceeds the city average, using a 40 percent threshold. It records SoHo-Little Italy at 30.5 percent.

The report scopes that definition to its own chapter. No source in this collection connects the threshold to the map, so the threshold is not treated here as an explanation for Little Italy’s absence.

What happened next

On July 10, 2026, amNewYork ↗ reported that the mayor’s team had updated the map. At the same press conference, the mayor said the map had been created by the prior administration in 2023 and said future changes would include adding Little Italy.

Checked on August 20, the known map file was unchanged from archived captures taken before the July reporting. The page that had carried the map redirected to the program index, while the image file was still served. No amended map was located.
